HK CUISINE | 港式飲食


Influenced by its history as a major trading port, Hong Kong's food culture signifies a unique blend of Eastern and Western cultures. We have selected a number of Hong Kong's signature cuisine and street food with recommendation on some tourist-must-try restaurants.

Cantonese cuisine is regarded as the backbone of Hong Kong's food culture. It is differentiated from other cuisine by its use of fresh ingredients, delicate flavors, and techniques like steaming and stir-frying. Dishes often feature seafood, poultry, and seasonal vegetables. One sub-category under Cantonese cuisine is Dim Sum, which is actually a variety of small dishes served in steamer baskets or on small plates. It's traditionally enjoyed during brunch and ranges from dumplings to buns and pastries. Some well-known Dim Sum are Shrimp dumpling, Siu Mai, Steamed Rice Noodle Roll and the Beef Ball. Apart from some formal delicacy, Hong Kong is also famous for its bustling street food scene, where vendors serve a variety of delicious and affordable snacks. The street food culture is a reflection of the city's fast-paced lifestyle and diverse palate. Some iconic street food includes Egg Waffles (Gai Daan Jai), a popular snack featuring a crispy exterior and soft, chewy interior, often enjoyed plain or filled with toppings like chocolate or fruit; Fish Balls made with real fish and served with curry sauce or soy sauce, making them a favorite street snack of many local people; Stinky Tofu, a fermented tofu dish with a strong odor that is deep-fried and served with pickled vegetables which is a must-try for adventurous foodie.

Another essential part of Hong Kong food culture is tea restaurant (Cha Chaan Teng), which embodies the city's blending of traditions and modernity, providing a snapshot of daily life and culinary diversity. Tea restaurants offer a wide range of dishes, including Hong Kong-style milk tea, instant noodles, as well as fusion dishes that reflect the city's colonial history. The prices are generally affordable, making them popular among locals for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. If you plan on visit a tea restaurant you must try its milk tea and egg tart. Hong Kong-style milk tea is known for its smooth, rich flavor because of its strong brew made from a blend of black teas, and often served with evaporated or condensed milk. A notable tea restaurant is the Australia Dairy Company, where tourists can experience its quick service, as a miniature of the fast-paced lifestyle of Hong Kong residents.
